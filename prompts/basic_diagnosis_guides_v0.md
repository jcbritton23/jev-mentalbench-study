# MentalBench diagnosis guides: first review batch

Status: **frozen for the v1.1 holdout request**. The original entries were used
in the full v1 exploration run. The more complete phobia/OCD descriptions
below were added afterward, checked on three related exploration variants,
and retained without a claim of improved accuracy. These are paraphrased,
decision-relevant guide entries for 11 of the 23 labels. The existing
[D013 MDD example](d013_mdd_sample_v0.md) supplies the twelfth. Each entry is
intended to accompany the same type of Noul question as D013: *Does the case
support retaining this named label as a credible candidate, considering the
available evidence and other available diagnoses?* The guide is context for a
fuzzy judgment, not a checklist of separate Jev questions or a clinical tool.
The original entries were drafted without MentalBench case text. The D012/D016
expansions followed an exploration-case audit. No holdout case or answer was
used to write the guides.

Across all entries: unreported facts are unknown, not automatically absent;
explicit contrary evidence weighs against a label. Required duration and
feature thresholds inform the score without making an incomplete vignette an
automatic zero. Distress/impairment matters. Consider a substance or medical
explanation if the vignette actually supplies one. These are benchmark labels,
not a claim that the diagnoses are always mutually exclusive in practice.

## D011 — Generalized Anxiety Disorder

- **Pattern:** Excessive, hard-to-control worry across multiple areas of life,
  rather than fear confined to one object or situation. Associated tension,
  restlessness, fatigue, poor concentration, irritability, or sleep disturbance
  can support the pattern.
- **Course:** Worry on more days than not for roughly six months or longer,
  with significant distress or interference, is the formal pattern. For
  adults, the DSM pattern includes at least three of six accompanying
  features: restlessness, fatigue, concentration difficulty, irritability,
  muscle tension, or sleep disturbance (one is sufficient for children).
- **Distinctions:** A circumscribed feared object favors specific phobia;
  trauma-linked re-experiencing and avoidance favor a trauma disorder; intrusive
  obsession plus ritualized response favors OCD. Do not equate ordinary
  situational worry with GAD.

## D012 — Specific Phobia

- **Pattern:** Marked fear or anxiety focused on a particular object or
  situation (for example, an animal, heights, flying, enclosed spaces, or
  injections). Exposure almost always triggers fear promptly; the person
  avoids the trigger or endures it with intense distress. The fear is out of
  proportion to the actual danger and context.
- **Course and impact:** The pattern is typically persistent for six months
  or more and causes clinically significant distress or interference. A
  brief, developmentally typical fear or reasonable caution about real danger
  is not enough.
- **Distinctions:** Identify what specifically triggers the fear and what the
  person expects will happen. Broad, shifting worry favors GAD; fear of
  scrutiny favors social anxiety; fear of panic or difficulty escaping may
  point to panic/agoraphobia; trauma reminders with re-experiencing suggest a
  trauma disorder. OCD is more likely when intrusive obsessions and/or
  repetitive rituals or mental acts form a distinct pattern. Judge whether
  another disorder better explains the fear rather than treating every
  avoidance behavior as phobia.

## D014 — Persistent Depressive Disorder

- **Pattern:** Chronic depressed mood with a prolonged low-mood course and
  associated changes such as low energy, sleep/appetite disturbance, poor
  concentration, low self-esteem, or hopelessness.
- **Course:** For adults, depressed mood most of the day on more days than not
  for at least two years, without a symptom-free interval exceeding two months;
  one year is the child/adolescent duration. The formal pattern includes at
  least two of six associated features: appetite change, sleep change, low
  energy, low self-esteem, poor concentration/indecisiveness, or hopelessness.
  Major depressive episodes can occur during this chronic course.
- **Distinctions:** A discrete two-week depressive episode without a long
  background course favors D013. Do not make D014 impossible merely because a
  current episode also reaches major-depression intensity. Manic/hypomanic
  history shifts the mood-disorder differential.

## D016 — Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

- **Pattern:** Obsessions are recurrent, persistent, unwanted thoughts, urges,
  or images that intrude and usually cause distress; the person may try to
  ignore, suppress, or neutralize them. Compulsions are repetitive behaviors
  or mental acts the person feels driven to perform, often in response to an
  obsession or rigid rule, to reduce distress or prevent a feared event. The
  response is excessive or not realistically connected to what it is meant to
  prevent. Obsessions, compulsions, or both may be present.
- **Course and impact:** Obsessions/compulsions are time-consuming (often
  more than an hour daily) or cause significant distress or impairment. The
  pattern should not be better explained by a substance, medical condition,
  or another disorder.
- **Distinctions:** Ordinary worries about plausible real-life problems are
  not automatically obsessions. A chiefly perceived appearance flaw points
  toward D017; trauma re-experiencing is not automatically an obsession.
  Specific phobia centers on a circumscribed trigger and its immediate fear
  response; fear-driven avoidance by itself does not establish OCD. Insight
  can range from good to absent, so poor insight alone does not prove
  psychosis.

## D017 — Body Dysmorphic Disorder

- **Pattern:** Persistent preoccupation with a perceived appearance defect
  that is unnoticeable or slight to others, accompanied by repetitive checking,
  grooming, reassurance seeking, comparison, or mental acts; distress or
  impairment is meaningful.
- **Distinctions:** Concern chiefly about body fat or weight in an eating
  disorder should not be relabeled BDD. Intrusions/rituals about other themes
  favor OCD. A realistic concern about an obvious injury or defect is not the
  defining BDD pattern. Level of insight may be poor or absent.

## D018 — Posttraumatic Stress Disorder

- **Pattern:** After qualifying exposure to actual/threatened death, serious
  injury, or sexual violence, a trauma-linked pattern includes intrusion,
  avoidance, negative cognition/mood changes, and altered arousal/reactivity.
  The formal adult pattern needs at least one intrusion feature (such as
  unwanted memories, dreams, flashbacks, or cue reactivity), one avoidance
  feature (internal or external reminders), two negative-cognition/mood
  features (such as blame, negative beliefs, detachment, or diminished
  interest), and two arousal/reactivity features (such as hypervigilance,
  startle, irritability, risky behavior, poor sleep, or concentration trouble).
- **Course:** Symptoms persist more than one month and cause distress or
  impairment. They need to be linked to the traumatic exposure, not merely
  follow it in time.
- **Distinctions:** The first three days to one month after trauma favors D019
  if acute-stress symptoms are sufficient. A non-qualifying life stressor with
  disproportionate distress may fit D020. Depression or anxiety can coexist,
  but should not substitute for the trauma-specific pattern.

## D019 — Acute Stress Disorder

- **Pattern:** A substantial trauma-linked reaction after qualifying exposure,
  with symptoms drawn from intrusion, negative mood, dissociation, avoidance,
  and arousal. The formal DSM pattern uses nine or more symptoms across these
  groups rather than the PTSD cluster-by-cluster rule.
- **Course:** Begins/continues from three days through one month after trauma,
  with distress or impairment.
- **Distinctions:** Beyond a month, consider PTSD; before three days, do not
  assume a formal acute stress disorder. A stressful event alone, without the
  relevant symptom pattern, is insufficient. ASD is not a required precursor
  to later PTSD.

## D020 — Adjustment Disorder

- **Pattern:** Emotional or behavioral symptoms in response to an identifiable
  stressor, disproportionate to its context and/or significantly impairing,
  where another disorder does not better explain the presentation.
- **Course:** Symptoms begin within three months of the stressor and do not
  persist beyond six months after the stressor or its consequences end; an
  ongoing stressor can support a longer course.
- **Distinctions:** A full major depressive, trauma-specific, or other syndrome
  can be the better account even if stress preceded it. A major life stressor
  need not meet PTSD's qualifying-trauma definition. Avoid treating any
  understandable reaction to stress as a disorder.

## D021 — Anorexia Nervosa

- **Pattern:** Restriction of energy intake leading to significantly low body
  weight for age and developmental context, with intense fear of gaining weight
  or persistent weight-gain-impeding behavior and disturbed experience of body
  weight/shape or its importance to self-evaluation.
- **Distinctions:** Bingeing or purging can occur in anorexia; the presence of
  those behaviors does not automatically make D022 more apt when significantly
  low weight and the anorexia pattern are present. Severe restriction without
  low weight can still be clinically serious but is not automatically this
  benchmark label. Weight/shape-driven restriction differs from ARFID-like
  sensory/fear-of-consequence avoidance.

## D022 — Bulimia Nervosa

- **Pattern:** Recurrent episodes of objectively large food intake with loss of
  control, followed by recurrent inappropriate compensation (such as vomiting,
  laxatives, fasting, or excessive exercise); body shape/weight strongly
  influences self-evaluation.
- **Course:** Bingeing and compensation average at least weekly for three
  months in the formal pattern.
- **Distinctions:** Bingeing without regular compensation favors D023.
  Significantly low weight with the anorexia pattern and binge/purge behavior
  favors D021's binge-eating/purging subtype. Weight appearance alone does not
  decide bulimia; behavior and loss of control do.

## D023 — Binge-Eating Disorder

- **Pattern:** Recurrent objectively large eating episodes with loss of
  control and marked distress. At least three of five associated features:
  eating rapidly, eating until uncomfortably full, eating large amounts when
  not physically hungry, eating alone from embarrassment, or feeling disgusted,
  depressed, or very guilty afterward.
- **Course:** Binge episodes occur at least weekly for three months in the
  formal pattern.
- **Distinctions:** Regular compensatory behavior favors D022; a significantly
  low-weight anorexia pattern can take priority when bingeing occurs there.
  Overeating without loss of control or distress is not sufficient. Body-image
  concerns may occur but are not required for this label.
