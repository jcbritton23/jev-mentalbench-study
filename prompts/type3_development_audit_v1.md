# Type 3 development-only audit after the full v1 exploration run

Status: two **exploration** variants inspected; no held-out case was read or
sent, and no guide or question was changed in response. The review is a
clinical-pattern interpretation by Codex, **not** clinician adjudication of
the benchmark. It cannot establish whether the remaining variants in either
source group are similarly worded.

The full exploration results show that Type 3 both-key recovery varies greatly
by diagnosis pair. At a 0.40 cutoff, all-23/four-option recovery was 0/15 for
D012/D016 (specific phobia/OCD) and 2/15 versus 1/15 for D017/D021 (body
dysmorphic disorder/anorexia nervosa). Each set of 15 is one related source
group, not 15 independent cases. These were selected *because* they were
weak, so the audit is diagnostic, not an unbiased accuracy estimate.

- **Specific phobia/OCD:** One inspected variant centers on a circumscribed
  fear of confined crossings and immediate/anticipatory anxiety, with
  counting while enduring the situation. The four-option scores were 0.86
  for phobia and 0.10 for OCD. Counting in that setting does not by itself
  establish recurrent intrusive obsessions or a ritual performed to
  neutralize them. The [NIMH phobia overview](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/phobias-and-phobia-related-disorders)
  and [NIMH OCD overview](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/obsessive-compulsive-disorder-when-unwanted-thoughts-or-repetitive-behaviors-take-over)
  support treating these as different patterns. The case could still omit
  relevant OCD history; the point is that its second benchmark key is not
  clearly established by the visible vignette alone.
- **Body dysmorphic disorder/anorexia nervosa:** One inspected variant centers
  on perceived facial defect, mirror checking, and reassurance seeking; it
  also mentions weight/shape worry but does not describe restrictive eating,
  significantly low weight, or weight-gain-avoidant behavior. The four-option
  scores were 0.94 for BDD and 0.13 for anorexia. The [APA eating-disorder
  overview](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/eating-disorders/what-are-eating-disorders)
  describes anorexia as fear of weight gain/body-shape concerns driving
  extreme food restriction and low body weight. Missing facts leave
  uncertainty, but raising anorexia merely to match the second benchmark
  key would not be justified by this inspected text.

**Method implication:** Do not revise the DSM-informed guides or choose a
lower cutoff solely to force both keyed labels in these families. Preserve
the benchmark keys for scoring, and separately describe clinically ambiguous
or under-supported secondary labels. A prospective clinician review would
be needed to assert clinical correctness. The v1 guides remain candidates
for the frozen holdout prompt; the operating differential cutoff is still a
decision to make with John, and the full score curve should be reported.
